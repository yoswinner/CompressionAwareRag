## Abstract

**Abstract**—PostgreSQL extensions such as `pgvector` rely on cost-based query optimization to choose between Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) index scans and sequential scans for top-$K$ vector search. Because PostgreSQL's `cost_seqscan` prices only main-heap blocks (`pg_class.relpages`) and omits out-of-line TOAST (`pg_toast`) pages, `pgvector` 0.8.4 discounts HNSW startup cost in `hnswcostestimate` whenever estimated startup pages exceed `relpages` and the traversal ratio is below `0.5`. We combine a source-level cost-model reconstruction of PostgreSQL 18.4 and `pgvector` 0.8.4 with benchmarks across three 768-dimensional representations, nine cost profiles, three primary scales ($1{,}001$, $5{,}000$, $10{,}000$ rows), and an 18-scale sweep ($500$–$5{,}000$ rows, `486` configurations). Under explicitly configured `STORAGE EXTENDED`, tested 32-bit `vector(768)` embeddings (`fp32_extended`) experienced zero compression reduction and moved to `pg_toast`, leaving `7` main-heap pages at $1{,}001$ rows. Inline storage via 16-bit `halfvec(768)` (`fp16_extended`, `201` pages) or `STORAGE PLAIN` (`fp32_plain`, `501` pages) raises `relpages` above HNSW startup pages, deactivating the discount and leaving unadjusted startup costs (`40.69×` and `80.91×` higher at Profile `P4`). Across the `486`-configuration sweep, the ratio threshold activates between $786$ and $787$ rows on `fp32_extended` (reducing `P4` HNSW startup cost by `88.06×` displayed / `88.08×` unrounded), `11/27` settings select sequential scans at $1{,}001$ rows (`2.90×` median slowdown on `fp32_extended` at `P7`; benign on `fp16_extended` at `P4`), and all `27/27` select HNSW by $5{,}000$ rows. End-to-end `halfvec(768)` cuts total storage by `55.21%–55.37%` and index size by `49.90%–49.98%`, achieving `0.985`, `0.960`, and `0.935` `Recall@10` under `P4` forced HNSW versus `0.985`, `0.955`, and `0.935` for `fp32_extended` (`0.985`, `0.955`, and `0.930` for `fp32_plain`).

*Keywords*—PostgreSQL, pgvector, query optimization, HNSW indexing, TOAST storage, half-precision floating-point (FP16).

## I. Introduction

Relational database management systems (RDBMSs) increasingly support vector similarity retrieval alongside scalar relational data for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and semantic search workloads [1], [2], [6], [7]. Rather than maintaining a separate vector store, deployments frequently integrate Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) graph indexes [5] directly into PostgreSQL through extensible access methods such as `pgvector` [2], [3] or PASE [6]. Inside a cost-based RDBMS, every top-$K$ similarity query (`ORDER BY embedding <=> $1 LIMIT K`) is governed by the query optimizer, which estimates page I/O and CPU operator costs across competing access paths (`Index Scan` versus `Seq Scan` with a bounded top-$K$ heap sort) before selecting an execution plan [4], [7], [24].

A fundamental cross-layer interaction arises because PostgreSQL's core sequential scan estimator (`cost_seqscan` in `costsize.c`) prices only the main-heap block count (`pg_class.relpages`) while omitting out-of-line pages stored in The Oversized-Attribute Storage Technique (`pg_toast`) [4], [21]. On standard 8 KiB (`8,192 B`) PostgreSQL pages, tuples exceeding the `2,032 B` TOAST threshold (`TOAST_TUPLE_THRESHOLD`, derived in Section II-A) are eligible for out-of-line storage [4]. In our benchmark, table provisioning explicitly configures `STORAGE EXTENDED` (which attempts inline lossless compression before externalizing oversized attributes) alongside `STORAGE PLAIN`. For our tested 768-dimensional 32-bit floating-point embeddings (`vector(768)`, `3,080 B` datum; `fp32_extended`), inline compression under `STORAGE EXTENDED` produced zero byte reduction on every row (`3,076.0 B` external `pg_column_size`), so PostgreSQL externalizes 100% of the vectors uncompressed into `pg_toast`. Consequently, the main table heap retains only compact 18-byte TOAST pointers (`varatt_external`) plus tuple headers (~157 tuples per full 8 KiB page), yielding a very small main-heap page count (`pg_class.relpages = 7` at $N = 1{,}001$ rows, `32` at $N = 5{,}000$, and `64` at $N = 10{,}000$).

To prevent `cost_seqscan`'s omission of `pg_toast` pages from making HNSW index scans appear artificially more expensive than sequential scans on TOASTed tables, `pgvector` 0.8.4 (`src/hnsw.c`, lines 187–194) implements an intentional TOAST-compensation heuristic in `hnswcostestimate` (`/* Adjust cost if needed since TOAST not included in seq scan cost */`) [3]. Whenever the estimated HNSW startup page count exceeds the main-heap page count and the estimated graph traversal ratio is below one-half (`startupPages > path->indexinfo->rel->pages && ratio < 0.5`), `hnswcostestimate` discounts the index startup cost by re-weighting `startupPages` from random to sequential page cost and subtracting the cost of pages beyond `relpages`. Crucially, however, `hnswcostestimate` keys this guard solely on main-heap `relpages` (`path->indexinfo->rel->pages`) rather than checking whether the vector column actually resides in `pg_toast`.

Consequently, two common physical design choices that keep 768-dimensional vectors inline within the main table heap alter how `hnswcostestimate` and `cost_seqscan` interact (Section II-A): (1) overriding the column storage policy to `STORAGE PLAIN` on single-precision vectors (`fp32_plain`, `3,080 B` datum, packing 2 tuples per 8 KiB page and expanding `relpages` to `501`, `2,500`, and `5,000`), or (2) representing coordinates as 16-bit half-precision floating-point values (`halfvec(768)` in `pgvector` [2], using IEEE-754 `binary16` format [25] under `STORAGE EXTENDED`, `fp16_extended`, `1,544 B` datum), which stays below the `2,032 B` TOAST threshold and packs 5 tuples per 8 KiB main-heap page (`relpages = 201`, `1,000`, and `2,000`). Moving vectors from out-of-line TOAST storage to inline main-heap storage raises `relpages` above `startupPages`, deactivating `pgvector`'s TOAST-compensation guard ($\Delta = 0$) and leaving the unadjusted generic HNSW startup estimate while `cost_seqscan` prices the full inline heap.

### A. Study Positioning and Research Questions

In the PostgreSQL and `pgvector` engineering communities, it is known that `cost_seqscan` ignores `pg_toast` pages [4], that `hnswcostestimate` includes a `relpages`-based adjustment to compensate for this omission [3], and that detoasting out-of-line FP32 vectors during sequential scans incurs multi-block buffer access overhead per row [21]. Peer-reviewed database studies have separately examined extensible ANN indexing in PostgreSQL [6], buffer-pool indirection and page-layout overheads relative to standalone Faiss [7], hybrid filtered vector execution [8], [9], cardinality estimation for vector range queries in analytical joins [10], and SSD-aware HNSW I/O [11]. Complementary to these works, this paper provides a closed-form C-source-to-`EXPLAIN` mathematical reconstruction and a controlled 18-scale empirical characterization showing how `pgvector`'s `relpages`-keyed guard (`startupPages > relpages && ratio < 0.5`), PostgreSQL's `cost_seqscan` and fuzzy path comparator (`compare_path_costs_fuzzily`), and inline storage via `halfvec(768)` or `STORAGE PLAIN` jointly govern HNSW versus `Seq Scan` crossover intervals across optimizer cost profiles.

We investigate three specific research questions:
- **RQ1 (Cost-Model Mechanics, Guard Regions, and 18-Scale Crossover Intervals):** How do vector representation (`vector(768)` vs. `halfvec(768)`) and column storage policy (`STORAGE EXTENDED` vs. `STORAGE PLAIN`) interact with `pgvector` 0.8.4's `hnswcostestimate` and PostgreSQL 18.4's `LIMIT` and fuzzy cost-comparison model across nine planner cost profiles (`P1`–`P9`), three primary benchmark scales ($N \in \{1{,}001, 5{,}000, 10{,}000\}$), and a verified 18-scale plan-only sweep ($N \in [500, 5{,}000]$, `486` configurations)?
- **RQ2 (Physical Storage, Shared-Buffer Traffic, and Client Round-Trip Latency):** What are the resulting trade-offs in physical relation and index byte footprints, root-node 8 KiB shared-buffer block accesses (including correlation with PostgreSQL's `BAS_BULKREAD` threshold), and client-observed query round-trip latency ($P_{50}, P_{95}$, outlier sensitivity, and non-parametric query-level inference)?
- **RQ3 (Retrieval Agreement and Boundary Tie Semantics):** How does primary conventional `Recall@10` relative to the exhaustive exact FP32 reference set behave across exact sequential scans and forced HNSW traversal, and how do exact duplicate-vector equivalence classes differ from genuine $\epsilon$-tolerance near-ties?

### B. Summary of Contributions and Scope Boundaries

1. **Closed-Form Cost-Model Reconstruction and Full 486-Cell Candidate Validation:** We reconstruct the six-step execution sequence in `pgvector` 0.8.4 (`hnswcostestimate` in `src/hnsw.c` [3]) and its three-level propagation through PostgreSQL 18.4 (`genericcostestimate`, `cost_index`, `index_pages_fetched`, `cost_seqscan`, `cost_tuplesort`, `create_limit_path`, and `compare_path_costs_fuzzily` [4], [22]). We validate our closed-form `Seq Scan + Top-k Sort` equation against live forced `exact`-mode `EXPLAIN` captures across all 486 configurations ($18 \times 3 \times 9$), matching PostgreSQL's displayed startup and total costs with `0.0000` maximum difference (i.e., identical at two-decimal `EXPLAIN` precision).
2. **18-Scale Planner Crossover Characterization:** Using the distinct `(scale, representation, profile)` setting (`27` per scale; `486` across the 18-scale sweep) as the primary unit for planner decisions, we separate the analytical `hnswcostestimate` guard boundary from the actual optimizer crossover. For $m = 16, ef_{\text{search}} = 40$, `ratio < 0.5` activates between $N = 786$ (`0.500041`) and $N = 787$ (`0.499493`), reducing `fp32_extended` Profile P4 HNSW startup cost by `88.06×` in displayed `EXPLAIN` units (`788.15 -> 8.95`; `88.08×` unrounded) on a same-scale basis (`87.93×` display / `87.95×` unrounded across $N = 786 \to 787$) and reducing root `Limit` total cost from `797.45` to `29.31`, flipping `7/9` profiles of `fp32_extended` to `Index Scan`. Inline `fp32_plain` and `fp16_extended` never activate the guard (`0/162` sweep cells each) and instead cross over to HNSW across $N \in [500, 5{,}000]$ as linear `Seq Scan + Sort` cost overtakes logarithmic unadjusted HNSW cost (including a `1%` `STD_FUZZ_FACTOR` total-cost tie-break at $N = 850$, `fp32_plain`, `P6`, where HNSW root `Limit` total cost `846.04` and `Seq Scan + Sort` total cost `843.86` lie within `1%` and HNSW wins on lower startup cost `814.52 < 843.36`, below the `1%` fuzzy startup threshold `843.36 / 1.01 = 835.01`).
3. **Storage, Buffer, Latency, and Retrieval Characterization:** We show that `fp16_extended` cuts total storage by `55.21%–55.37%` and HNSW index size by `49.90%–49.98%`, avoids `fp32_extended`'s `relpages + 3N` sequential-scan TOAST buffer amplification, and stays below PostgreSQL's `NBuffers / 4 = 4,096` block `BAS_BULKREAD` threshold at $N = 10{,}000$. Crucially, inline storage does not automatically induce a harmful plan: at $N = 1{,}001$, `fp16_extended`'s Profile `P4` `Seq Scan` choice has no statistically significant client-latency penalty (`1.674 ms` vs. `1.591 ms` $P_{50}$, $p_{\text{adj}} = 0.2024$), whereas the physically harmful tested fallback is TOASTed `fp32_extended` at `P7` (`2.90×` client median slowdown: `4.935 ms` vs. `1.701 ms`). Separating HNSW index execution advantages from FP16 representation effects, forced HNSW index scans deliver large client-latency speedups over exact sequential scans at $N \ge 5{,}000$ across all three representations (`15.76×` and `33.86×` median speedups on `fp32_extended`, `3.31×` and `18.59×` on `fp32_plain`, and `2.29×` and `5.15×` on `fp16_extended`, where the smaller ratio on `fp16_extended` reflects its faster inline exact scan). Under exact scans, `fp16_extended` matches `599/600` top-10 FP32 reference slots (`1.000 / 0.995 / 1.000` conventional `Recall@10`, with a 1-slot swap on `Q17` at $N = 5{,}000$), and under P4 `forced_hnsw` it achieves `0.985 / 0.960 / 0.935` conventional `Recall@10` versus `0.985 / 0.955 / 0.935` on `fp32_extended` and `0.985 / 0.955 / 0.930` on `fp32_plain` (`0.9737` and `0.9684` at $N = 10{,}000$ excluding a 28-chunk exact vector-equality cluster on `Q9`, detailed in Appendix A).

**Scope Boundaries:** This is an observational study of unmodified PostgreSQL `18.4` and `pgvector` `0.8.4` over unfiltered single-worker (`max_parallel_workers_per_gather = 0`) top-10 cosine queries ($d = 768$, $m = 16$, $ef_{\text{construction}} = 64$, $ef_{\text{search}} = 40$) using a prepared `BeIR/msmarco` collection [14], [15] and 20 fixed topical queries (`q0`–`q19`) in a warm-cache regime. Because no human relevance judgments (`qrels`) are used, `Recall@10` measures candidate agreement with exact FP32 cosine ranking rather than judged IR relevance.

## II. Background and Source-Level Cost-Model Analysis

### A. PostgreSQL Tuple Storage, `TOAST_TUPLE_THRESHOLD = 2,032 B`, and `relpages`

PostgreSQL stores relation data in 8 KiB (`BLCKSZ = 8,192 B`) heap pages, each with a 24-byte `PageHeaderData` and 4-byte `ItemIdData` line pointers [4]. In `src/include/access/heaptoast.h` and `htup_details.h`, PostgreSQL defines the compile-time tuple-level TOAST threshold by requiring at least `TOAST_TUPLES_PER_PAGE = 4` tuples per page, and sets the default relation-level `toast_tuple_target` parameter to `TOAST_TUPLE_TARGET = TOAST_TUPLE_THRESHOLD`:
$$\text{TOAST\_TUPLE\_THRESHOLD} = \text{MaximumBytesPerTuple}(4) = \text{MAXALIGN\_DOWN}\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac{8192 - \text{MAXALIGN}(24 + 4 \times 4)}{4}\right\rfloor\right) = \mathbf{2{,}032\text{ B}} \tag{1}$$
For a table that does not override `toast_tuple_target`, this `2,032 B` threshold is compared against the uncompressed heap tuple length `t_len`. In our two-column experimental schema (`CREATE TABLE chunks_{scale}_{rep} (chunk_id INT PRIMARY KEY, embedding {vector(768)|halfvec(768)} NOT NULL)` in the provisioning DDL; see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B), both columns are `NOT NULL` (`hasnull = false`), so `SizeofHeapTupleHeader` (`23 B`) aligns to `t_hoff = 24 B`, `chunk_id` occupies `4 B` (bytes `24..27`), and under effective 4-byte attribute alignment (`typalign = 'i'`) the `embedding` datum starts at byte offset `28` (`t_len = 28 + VARSIZE`), while 8-byte page-slot alignment (`MAXALIGN(t_len)`) adds `4 B` of trailing padding (`32 B` combined header/key/page-alignment overhead before adding the `4 B` `ItemIdData` line pointer) [4]:

1. **`vector(768)` under `STORAGE PLAIN` (`fp32_plain`):** A 768-D IEEE-754 `binary32` vector [2], [25] occupies $768 \times 4\text{ B} = 3{,}072\text{ B}$ plus a 4-byte `varlena` header (`VARHDRSZ = 4`), 2-byte `dim`, and 2-byte `unused` field, totaling $\text{VARSIZE} = 4d + 8 = 3{,}080\text{ B}$ (`pg_column_size = 3,080 B`). This yields a raw heap tuple length of $t_{\text{len}} = 24 + 4 + 3{,}080 = 3{,}108\text{ B}$ and an 8-byte aligned page-slot footprint of $\text{MAXALIGN}(t_{\text{len}}) = 3{,}112\text{ B}$ (plus a `4 B` `ItemIdData` line pointer = `3,116 B` per page slot). Setting `STORAGE PLAIN` disables compression and out-of-line TOAST chunk storage (`toast_table_bytes = 0 B`, `0` chunk pages), packing **2 tuples per 8 KiB page** (`relpages = 501`, `2,500`, and `5,000` at $N = 1{,}001, 5{,}000$, and $10{,}000$). Because our table-provisioning workflow (see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B) executes `CREATE TABLE` before executing `ALTER TABLE ... ALTER COLUMN embedding SET STORAGE PLAIN`, PostgreSQL allocates an associated `pg_toast` relation and its mandatory 1-page B-tree index metapage (`toast_index_bytes = 8,192 B`) at `CREATE TABLE` time when `embedding` inherits its type-level TOAST-eligible storage policy (`STORAGE EXTERNAL` [2]); the subsequent `SET STORAGE PLAIN` statement prevents `INSERT` from writing TOAST chunks (`toast_table_bytes = 0 B`) without dropping the already-created `pg_toast` relation, yielding $\text{toast\_total\_bytes} = \text{toast\_table\_bytes} + \text{toast\_index\_bytes} = 0 + 8{,}192 = 8{,}192\text{ B}$ (`1` page).
2. **`vector(768)` under `STORAGE EXTENDED` (`fp32_extended`):** Our provisioning workflow explicitly sets `ALTER COLUMN embedding SET STORAGE EXTENDED` (PostgreSQL's general `varlena` default, whereas `pgvector` 0.8.4's type-level storage policy is `STORAGE EXTERNAL` [2]). Because $t_{\text{len}} = 3{,}108\text{ B} > 2{,}032\text{ B}$, `toast_tuple_try_compression` attempts inline lossless compression (`pglz` / `lz4`); across our tested corpus (see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B), compression produced zero byte reduction on any row (`min = max = avg_col_size_bytes = 3,076.0 B`). Consequently, `toast_save_datum` (`src/backend/access/common/toast_internals.c`) externalizes every attribute uncompressed to `pg_toast` as two chunks per vector (packing 4 chunks, or 2 vectors, per 8 KiB TOAST chunk page) and replaces the main-heap attribute with an 18-byte external TOAST pointer (`varatt_external`). At $N = 1{,}001$, the external TOAST chunk heap occupies $\text{toast\_table\_bytes} = 4{,}104{,}192\text{ B}$ (`501` pages) and its B-tree chunk index occupies $\text{toast\_index\_bytes} = 65{,}536\text{ B}$ (`8` pages), producing $\text{toast\_total\_bytes} = \text{toast\_table\_bytes} + \text{toast\_index\_bytes} = 4{,}169{,}728\text{ B}$ (`509` pages). When writing an uncompressed external datum of size `VARSIZE = 3,080 B`, `toast_save_datum` strips the 4-byte `VARHDRSZ` header and stores `VARSIZE - VARHDRSZ = 3,076 B` in `toast_pointer.va_extinfo`, which `pg_column_size()` (`dbsize.c`) returns for on-disk TOAST pointers (`3,076 B`). In the main heap, compact pointer tuples (`t_len = 46 B`, `MAXALIGN = 48 B`, plus `4 B` `ItemIdData` = `52 B` per page slot) pack at **approximately `157` tuples per full 8 KiB page** across our 18-scale corpus (`relpages = 7` at $N = 1{,}001$ [6 full pages + 1 partial page], `32` at $N = 5{,}000$, and `64` at $N = 10{,}000$).
3. **`halfvec(768)` under `STORAGE EXTENDED` (`fp16_extended`):** Storing each coordinate in IEEE-754 `binary16` format [25] via `pgvector`'s `halfvec(768)` type [2] reduces the datum size (`pg_column_size`) to $2d + 8 = 1{,}544\text{ B}$, the raw heap tuple length to $t_{\text{len}} = 24 + 4 + 1{,}544 = 1{,}572\text{ B}$, and the 8-byte aligned page-slot footprint to $\text{MAXALIGN}(t_{\text{len}}) = 1{,}576\text{ B}$ (plus a `4 B` `ItemIdData` line pointer = `1,580 B` per page slot). Because $t_{\text{len}} = 1{,}572\text{ B} \le 2{,}032\text{ B}$ (and $\text{MAXALIGN}(t_{\text{len}}) = 1{,}576\text{ B} \le 2{,}032\text{ B}$), PostgreSQL never invokes TOAST compression or out-of-line chunk storage (`toast_table_bytes = 0 B`, `0` chunk pages, identical under `STORAGE EXTENDED` and `STORAGE EXTERNAL`), while the `pg_toast` relation allocated at `CREATE TABLE` time retains only its 1-page B-tree index metapage (`toast_index_bytes = 8,192 B`), giving $\text{toast\_total\_bytes} = 0 + 8{,}192 = 8{,}192\text{ B}$. Since $5 \times 1{,}580\text{ B} = 7{,}900\text{ B} \le 8{,}168\text{ B}$, `fp16_extended` packs **5 tuples per 8 KiB main-heap page** (`relpages = 201`, `1,000`, and `2,000` at $N = 1{,}001, 5{,}000$, and $10{,}000$).

PostgreSQL's `ANALYZE` populates `pg_class.relpages` (`path->indexinfo->rel->pages`) strictly with main-heap blocks, omitting `pg_toast` [4].

### B. Six-Step Execution Sequence in `pgvector` 0.8.4 `hnswcostestimate`

Reconciling `pgvector` 0.8.4 (`src/hnsw.c` [3]) and PostgreSQL 18.4 (`src/backend/utils/adt/selfuncs.c` [4]) against our captured `EXPLAIN` plan trees establishes the six-step cost computation for an unfiltered top-$K$ query (`ORDER BY embedding <=> $1 LIMIT 10`):

**Step 1 — Zero-Initialization:** `hnswcostestimate` zeroes `GenericCosts costs` (`MemSet(&costs, 0, sizeof(costs))`), leaving `costs.numIndexTuples = 0.0`.

**Step 2 — Generic Index Estimate (`genericcostestimate`):** Because `costs.numIndexTuples == 0` and `path->indexclauses == NIL`, `genericcostestimate` sets `*indexSelectivity = 1.0`, clamps `costs.numIndexTuples` to `index->tuples = N`, sets `costs.numIndexPages = index->pages`, and computes the full-index scan cost (`cpu_index_tuple_cost = 0.005`):
$$\text{costs.indexTotalCost} = \text{numIndexPages} \cdot \text{spc\_random\_page\_cost} + N \cdot (\text{cpu\_index\_tuple\_cost} + \text{cpu\_operator\_cost}) \tag{2}$$
At $N = 1{,}001$, Profile P4 (`random_page_cost = 4.0`, `seq_page_cost = 1.0`, `cpu_operator_cost = 0.0025`, `cpu_tuple_cost = 0.01`), the full-index CPU term is $1001 \times 0.0075 = 7.5075$. With `numIndexPages = 499` for FP32 (`1996.0` page I/O cost) and `250` for FP16 (`1000.0` page I/O cost), (2) gives $\text{indexTotalCost}_{\text{FP32}} = 1996.0 + 7.5075 = 2003.5075$ (**`2003.51`**) and $\text{indexTotalCost}_{\text{FP16}} = 1000.0 + 7.5075 = 1007.5075$ (**`1007.51`**).

**Step 3 — HNSW Traversal Selectivity (`ratio`):** `hnswcostestimate` models the fraction of index tuples visited during greedy upper-layer descent ($\text{entryLevel}(N) = \lfloor \ln N / \ln m \rfloor$, which equals $2$ for $256 \le N < 4{,}096$ and $3$ for $4{,}096 \le N < 65{,}536$ at $m = 16$) and layer-0 beam search (`hnsw.ef_search = 40`):
$$\text{candidateTuples}(N) = \text{entryLevel}(N) \cdot m + C_{\text{cal}}(m, ef_{\text{search}}) \cdot \ln N, \qquad \text{ratio}(N) = \frac{\text{candidateTuples}(N)}{N} \tag{3}$$
For $m = 16, ef_{\text{search}} = 40$, the empirically calibrated layer-0 scaling coefficient $C_{\text{cal}}(16, 40) = 54.152441134$ (`LAYER0_TUPLE_SCALING_CONST`, calibrated from captured `EXPLAIN` plans at $N = 1{,}001$) reproduces the observed unadjusted startup costs across all 18 scales ($N \in [500, 5{,}000]$) to within $\le 0.01$ cost units. Notice that $\text{ratio}(N)$ depends only on $N, m,$ and $ef_{\text{search}}$ (not on dimension $d$ or storage policy) and crosses `0.50` between **$N = 786$ ($\text{ratio} = 0.500041 \ge 0.50$)** and **$N = 787$ ($\text{ratio} = 0.499493 < 0.50$)**; at $N = 1{,}001, 5{,}000,$ and $10{,}000$, $\text{ratio}(N)$ equals **`0.405720`**, **`0.101845`**, and **`0.054676`**.

**Step 4 — Unadjusted Generic HNSW Startup Estimate:** `hnswcostestimate` scales `costs.indexTotalCost` by `ratio`:
$$\text{indexStartupCost}_{\text{initial}} = \text{costs.indexTotalCost} \cdot \text{ratio} = (\text{numIndexPages} \cdot \text{spc\_random\_page\_cost}) \cdot \text{ratio} + \text{indexCPUCost}_{\text{total}} \cdot \text{ratio} \tag{4}$$
At Profile P4 ($N = 1{,}001$), using the 8-decimal calibrated ratio $\text{ratio}_{\text{cal}} = 0.40572022$ (`0.4057202163` full precision, where the startup CPU term is $7.5075 \times 0.40572022 = 3.045945 \approx 3.0459$), (4) evaluates to:
$$\text{indexStartupCost}_{\text{initial, FP32}} = 1996.0 \times 0.40572022 + 3.045945 = 809.817559 + 3.045945 = \mathbf{812.8635} \ (\mathbf{812.86}\text{ in }\texttt{EXPLAIN})$$
$$\text{indexStartupCost}_{\text{initial, FP16}} = 1000.0 \times 0.40572022 + 3.045945 = 405.720220 + 3.045945 = \mathbf{408.7662} \ (\mathbf{408.77}\text{ in }\texttt{EXPLAIN})$$
*(Note: Truncating `ratio` to 6 decimal places as `0.405720` with 4-decimal intermediate rounding `startupPages = 202.4543` and `101.4300` and CPU term `3.0460` [see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B] gives $809.8172 + 3.0460 = 812.8632$ and $405.7200 + 3.0460 = 408.7660$; both parameterizations round to the exact same two-decimal captured `EXPLAIN` startup costs of `812.86` and `408.77`.)*

**Step 5 — Estimated Startup Pages (`startupPages`):** `startupPages = costs.numIndexPages * ratio`, giving **`202.4544` pages** ($499 \times 0.40572022 = 202.454390$, or `202.4543` under 6-decimal `0.405720`) for FP32 and **`101.4301` pages** ($250 \times 0.40572022 = 101.430055$, or `101.4300` under `0.405720`) for FP16 at $N = 1{,}001$.

**Step 6 — Intentional TOAST-Compensation Guard (`startupPages > relpages && ratio < 0.5`):** Finally, `src/hnsw.c` executes:
```c
/* Adjust cost if needed since TOAST not included in seq scan cost */
if (startupPages > path->indexinfo->rel->pages && ratio < 0.5)
{
    /* Change random page cost to sequential page cost */
    costs.indexStartupCost -= startupPages * (costs.spc_random_page_cost - spc_seq_page_cost);
    /* Remove cost of extra pages */
    costs.indexStartupCost -= (startupPages - path->indexinfo->rel->pages) * spc_seq_page_cost;
}
```
Combining both subtractions defines the TOAST-compensation startup discount $\Delta$:
$$\Delta = \text{startupPages} \cdot (\text{random\_page\_cost} - \text{seq\_page\_cost}) + (\text{startupPages} - \text{relpages}) \cdot \text{seq\_page\_cost} = \text{startupPages} \cdot \text{random\_page\_cost} - \text{relpages} \cdot \text{seq\_page\_cost} \tag{5}$$
When the guard is **True**, subtracting $\Delta$ cancels $\text{startupPages} \cdot \text{random\_page\_cost}$, leaving:
$$\text{indexStartupCost}_{\text{adjusted}} = \text{relpages} \cdot \text{seq\_page\_cost} + N \cdot (\text{cpu\_index\_tuple\_cost} + \text{cpu\_operator\_cost}) \cdot \text{ratio} \tag{6}$$
At $N = 1{,}001$ ($\text{ratio}_{\text{cal}} = 0.40572022 < 0.5$), `fp32_extended` (`relpages = 7 < 202.4544`) satisfies the guard ($\Delta = 607.3632 + 195.4544 = 802.8176$ under $\text{ratio}_{\text{cal}}$, or `802.8172` under `0.405720`; **`802.82`** at two decimals), reducing `*indexStartupCost` to $7 \times 1.0 + 3.045945 = \mathbf{10.0459}$ (**`10.05`** in `EXPLAIN` across `P1, P4, P7`). For inline `fp32_plain` (`relpages = 501`) and `fp16_extended` (`relpages = 201`), `startupPages <= relpages` disables the workaround ($\Delta = 0.00$), leaving the unadjusted generic HNSW startup estimates of **`812.86`** (`812.8635` calibrated / `812.8632` baseline, an **`80.91×` unrounded / `80.88×` display-rounded ratio** vs. `10.0459` / `10.05`) and **`408.77`** (`408.7662` calibrated / `408.7660` baseline, a **`40.69×` unrounded / `40.67×` display-rounded ratio**).

### C. Three-Level Cost Propagation, Competing `Seq Scan + Sort`, and `1%` Fuzzy Tie-Breaking

1. **Level 2 (`cost_index` and Mackert–Lohman `index_pages_fetched`):** In `costsize.c` [4], `cost_index` sets $\text{IndexScan}_{\text{startup}} = \text{*indexStartupCost}$. For an uncorrelated index (`indexCorrelation = 0.0`), `cost_index` computes random heap page fetches via `index_pages_fetched(N, T, index->pages, root)` (implementing the Mackert and Lohman LRU buffer model [22]): when $T = \text{relpages} \le b$, $\text{pages\_fetched} = \lceil \min(T, 2TN/(2T+N)) \rceil$, which evaluates to $T = \text{relpages}$ whenever $2TN/(2T+N) > T - 1 \iff \mathbf{N > 2T(T-1)/(T+1)}$. Across all 54 validated `(scale, representation)` storage configurations, `index_pages_fetched` evaluates identically to `relpages`. Thus:
   $$\text{IndexScan}_{\text{run}} = (\text{indexTotalCost} - \text{IndexScan}_{\text{startup}}) + \text{relpages} \cdot \text{random\_page\_cost} + N \cdot (\text{cpu\_tuple\_cost} + \text{cpu\_operator\_cost}) \tag{7}$$
   and $\text{IndexScan}_{\text{total}} = \text{IndexScan}_{\text{startup}} + \text{IndexScan}_{\text{run}}$ is invariant to $\Delta$ (`2044.02`, `4020.02`, and `1824.02` at $N = 1{,}001$, P4).
2. **Level 3 (`create_limit_path` for `LIMIT K`):** Scaling $\text{IndexScan}_{\text{run}}$ by $K/N$ ($K = 10$) propagates $\Delta_{\text{Limit}} = (1 - K/N)\Delta$ (`794.80` units at $N = 1{,}001$, P4) into the root `Limit` total cost:
   $$\text{Limit}_{\text{total, HNSW}} = \text{IndexScan}_{\text{startup}} + \frac{K}{N} \cdot \text{IndexScan}_{\text{run}} \tag{8}$$
   yielding Profile P4 root `Limit` total costs of **`30.37`** (`30.3654`) for `fp32_extended` versus **`844.90`** (`844.9027`, **`27.82×`**) for `fp32_plain` and **`422.90`** (`422.9044`, **`13.93×` unrounded / `13.92×` display**) for `fp16_extended`.
3. **Competing `Limit -> Sort -> Seq Scan` Path (`cost_seqscan` + `cost_tuplesort`):** Pricing main-heap sequential scan ($\text{relpages} \cdot \text{seq\_page\_cost} + N(\text{cpu\_tuple\_cost} + \text{cpu\_operator\_cost})$), bounded top-$K$ heapsort comparisons ($2.0 \cdot \text{cpu\_operator\_cost} \cdot N \log_2(2.0 K)$), and $K$ output tuples ($K \cdot \text{cpu\_operator\_cost}$) gives the exact closed-form equation:
   $$\text{Limit}_{\text{total, Seq}} = \text{relpages} \cdot \text{seq\_page\_cost} + N \cdot \big(\text{cpu\_tuple\_cost} + \text{cpu\_operator\_cost}\,[1 + 2\log_2(2K)]\big) + K \cdot \text{cpu\_operator\_cost} \tag{9}$$
   At $N = 1{,}001$ Profile P4, (9) yields **`41.17`** (`fp32_extended`), **`535.17`** (`fp32_plain`), and **`235.17`** (`fp16_extended`).
4. **Fuzzy Path Comparison (`compare_path_costs_fuzzily` in `pathnode.c`):** PostgreSQL compares candidate paths using `STD_FUZZ_FACTOR = 1.01` (`1%`) [4]. If two paths' total costs lie within `1%` ($\max(C_1, C_2) \le 1.01\min(C_1, C_2)$), they are considered fuzzily tied on `total_cost` and PostgreSQL selects the path with the lower `startup_cost` (when $S_{\text{winner}} < S_{\text{loser}} / 1.01$). ## III. Related Work

1. **PostgreSQL Vector Indexing and Practitioner TOAST Observations:** Yang et al. [6] introduced PASE for IVFFlat and HNSW indexing inside PostgreSQL, and `pgvector` [2], [3] implements exact vector scans, IVFFlat, and HNSW [5] with support for 16-bit `halfvec` attributes. Zhang, Liu, and Wang [7] compared PostgreSQL/PASE against standalone Faiss, identifying seven execution-engine root causes (`RC#1`–`RC#7`), including missing SGEMM in IVF build (`RC#1`), buffer-pool page indirection and tuple access overhead (`RC#2`), global top-$K$ heap locking (`RC#3`), and 8 KiB page-layout space amplification (`RC#4`). In a non-archival practitioner conference presentation at PGConf.EU 2025, Otochkin [21] noted that FP32 vectors exceeding ~2 KiB (`>500` dimensions) move to `pg_toast`, resist compression, and incur 3–4 buffer accesses per row during sequential KNN scans unless `STORAGE PLAIN` is used. Complementary to Zhang et al.'s [7] execution-engine study and Otochkin's [21] practitioner talk, we reconstruct `pgvector` 0.8.4's `hnswcostestimate` [3] and characterize how inline `halfvec(768)` and `STORAGE PLAIN` layouts interact with `startupPages > relpages && ratio < 0.5` across cost profiles and scales.
2. **Hybrid Vector-Relational Processing and Query Optimization:** Wei et al. [8] developed AnalyticDB-V to fuse structured predicates and vector search via selectivity-guided plan selection, and Zhang et al. [9] proposed VBASE to unify online vector similarity and relational queries via relaxed monotonicity. In classical relational optimization, Mackert and Lohman [22] validated the LRU buffer I/O model (`index_pages_fetched`), and Leis et al. [24] demonstrated that cardinality-estimation errors typically outweigh cost-model errors in multi-join plans. Extending cardinality estimation to vector-augmented analytical queries (VAQs) with multi-table joins and range predicates, Kim et al. [10] introduced Exqutor, combining Exact Cardinality Query Optimization (ECQO, via planning-time ANN range probing with result reuse) and Q-error-guided adaptive sampling. Whereas Leis et al. [24] and Exqutor [10] focus on join and range-predicate cardinality estimation, our study examines single-table top-$K$ ordering queries (`LIMIT 10`) where output cardinality is fixed ($K = 10$) and plan selection is governed jointly by relation scale ($N$), the calibrated HNSW entry-traversal ratio ($\text{ratio}(N) < 0.5$), physical storage layout (`relpages` under inline versus TOASTed storage), and planner cost settings (`random_page_cost`, `cpu_operator_cost`) inside `hnswcostestimate` and `cost_seqscan`.
3. **SSD-Aware HNSW I/O, Reduced Precision, and Tie-Aware Retrieval:** Shim et al. [11] optimized physical NVMe SSD I/O during forced `pgvector` HNSW searches via `io_uring` batching and colocation, whereas we analyze PostgreSQL 18.4 shared-buffer traffic and `BAS_BULKREAD` ring-buffer behavior across exact and unforced scans. Unlike codebook-based Product Quantization (PQ) [12], `pgvector`'s `halfvec(768)` [2] applies a direct scalar conversion to IEEE-754 `binary16` format [25], halving HNSW index size while keeping tuples below `TOAST_TUPLE_THRESHOLD`. For retrieval evaluation, we distinguish McSherry and Najork's [13] expected IR metrics over random permutations of exact score ties from Aumüller et al.'s [23] $\epsilon$-threshold ANN recall, treating exact duplicate vectors as an equivalence class and genuine $\epsilon$-near-ties as a secondary tolerance bound (Appendix A).

## IV. Experimental Methodology

### A. Dataset, Representations, and Verified Query `q0`–`q19` Provenance

We use a prepared passage collection from the `BeIR/msmarco` corpus split [14], [15] (`5,457` source documents segmented into `10,001` chunks of `300` characters with `50`-character overlap, source TSV SHA-256 `cdca54aa...34e8b`; the upstream filter selecting those `5,457` documents is not separately recorded; see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B). Three primary benchmark scales ($N \in \{1{,}001, 5{,}000, 10{,}000\}$) and sixteen intermediate sweep scales ($N \in \{500, 700, 750, 780, 785, 786, 787, 788, 790, 850, 1{,}250, 1{,}500, 1{,}750, 2{,}000, 3{,}000, 4{,}000\}$) are constructed as deterministic nested prefix slices (`ORDER BY chunk_id ASC LIMIT N`); because each scale $N$ is a prefix slice rather than an independent random sample, smaller scales are strict subsets of larger scales and corpus composition evolves monotonically with $N$. Passages and queries are represented as 768-dimensional embeddings using the mutable Ollama model tag `nomic-embed-text:latest` [16]. The exact model artifact digest and Ollama runtime build were not recorded; consequently, the original embedding-generation environment cannot be reconstructed exactly from the available provenance. At each scale $N$, three two-column tables (`chunks_{scale}_{rep}`) are provisioned with 100% `chunk_id` parity, explicit `ALTER COLUMN embedding SET STORAGE` policy assignment (see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B), a single HNSW index build ($m = 16, ef_{\text{construction}} = 64$), and immediate `ANALYZE`: (1) `fp32_extended` (`vector(768)`, `SET STORAGE EXTENDED`, `3,076 B` uncompressed external `pg_column_size`), (2) `fp32_plain` (`vector(768)`, `SET STORAGE PLAIN`, `3,080 B` inline datum), and (3) `fp16_extended` (`halfvec(768)`, `SET STORAGE EXTENDED`, `1,544 B` inline datum).

The workload comprises 20 fixed topical queries (`q0`–`q19`) pinned in the benchmark harness (`CompressionAwareRag` repository, `research/planner-sensitivity` branch, commit `3f2beee66cecd67aa8fc4cec69c364b92c07b884`; see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B). In particular, **canonical query `q0` (`query_id = 0`) is `"what was the purpose of the manhattan project"`** (retrieving the exact FP32 reference top-10 `chunk_id` sequence `[11, 4, 5, 14, 3, 10, 12, 9, 1, 7]` in rank order `1..10` across $N = 1{,}001, 5{,}000$, and $10{,}000$), whose precomputed 768-float embedding is verified by the UTF-8 JSON SHA-256 hash `4a0428709013d77d8d284b369cb144734421bfdc4f6e4a83faf17db8114cf478` (see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B). Likewise, `q8` (`query_id = 8`) is *"how do vector databases store and index embeddings"*, `q9` (`query_id = 9`) is *"difference between supervised and unsupervised machine learning"*, and `q17` (`query_id = 17`) is *"how do commercial airplane wings produce aerodynamic lift"*. All `fp16_extended` queries cast the query operand to `halfvec(768)` (`SELECT chunk_id, (embedding <=> %s::halfvec(768)) AS distance ... ORDER BY distance ASC LIMIT 10`), evaluating end-to-end 16-bit representation across both stored vectors and query operands.

### B. Hardware, PostgreSQL 18.4 Configuration, and Planner Profiles (`P1`–`P9`)

Experiments ran on a Windows 11 (`10.0.26200`, `x86-64`) host with an Intel Core Ultra 9 185H CPU (16 physical cores, 22 logical processors), 32 GB RAM (`15.08 GiB` free at snapshot), local NVMe storage, PostgreSQL `18.4`, `pgvector` `0.8.4`, Python `3.13.14`, and `psycopg` `3.3.4`. Verified PostgreSQL 18.4 server parameters (see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B) are `shared_buffers = 128MB` (`16,384` 8 KiB blocks), `effective_cache_size = 4GB`, `work_mem = 4MB`, `effective_io_concurrency = 16` (default), `io_combine_limit = 128kB` (`16` blocks, default), and server-default `max_parallel_workers_per_gather = 2`. Scale $N = 1{,}001$ resides in `compression_aware_rag_exp`, and scales $N \in \{5{,}000, 10{,}000\}$ plus the 16 intermediate sweep scales reside in `compression_aware_rag_scale` (`32/32` pre-existing catalog objects verified unchanged).

We evaluate a $3 \times 3$ grid of `random_page_cost` $\in \{1.0, 4.0, 8.0\}$ and `cpu_operator_cost` $\in \{0.0025, 0.0100, 0.0500\}$ (`P1`–`P3` at `rpc = 1.0`; `P4`–`P6` at `rpc = 4.0`; `P7`–`P9` at `rpc = 8.0`), holding `seq_page_cost = 1.0`, `cpu_tuple_cost = 0.01`, `cpu_index_tuple_cost = 0.005`, `hnsw.ef_search = 40`, and `LIMIT = 10` fixed. Importantly, the benchmark harness sets `SET LOCAL max_parallel_workers_per_gather = 0` in every transaction to isolate single-worker access-path selection; thus, **Profile `P4` (`rpc = 4.0, cpu_op = 0.0025`) uses PostgreSQL's baseline page and CPU cost parameters under single-worker execution**, rather than the full out-of-the-box parallel server configuration, and extreme settings (`rpc = 8.0`, `cpu_op = 0.05`) serve as controlled sensitivity probes of the optimizer's cost equations rather than typical deployment recommendations. Three execution modes are tested via `SET LOCAL`: `planner_selected` (unforced), `exact` (`enable_indexscan = off, enable_bitmapscan = off`), and `forced_hnsw` (`enable_seqscan = off`).

### C. Three-Stage Evaluation Protocol, 486-Cell Candidate-Cost Validation, and Timing Semantics

All queries execute inside read-only transactions with prepared-statement caching disabled (`prepare_threshold=None`, `prepare=False`):
1. **Deterministic Plan-Capture Stage (`4,860` plans across `81` unforced settings) and 18-Scale Sweep (`972` plans + `486` exact-validation plans across `486` settings):** In the 3-scale baseline ($3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 9 \times 20 = 4{,}860$ `EXPLAIN` captures), **0 of 243 cells** vary in plan or cost across `q0`–`q19`. Thus, the **distinct `(scale, representation, profile)` setting (`27` per scale; `486` across the 18-scale sweep)** is the primary unit for planner decisions, and deterministic plan counts (`972` or `486`) are never treated as independent sample observations in statistical inference. In the 18-scale sweep (see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B; using `q0` SHA-256 `4a042870...`), component timers recorded `5.750 s` (provisioning 48 tables), `31.670 s` (48 HNSW builds + `ANALYZE`), and `5.057 s` (`972` `EXPLAIN` captures in `planner_selected` and `forced_hnsw` modes); their sum (`42.477 s`) is an arithmetic sum of discrete stage timers, whereas continuous end-to-end wall-clock runtime is marked `unavailable` (total multi-stage workflow span `622.086 s`, finalization pass `2.086 s`). In our 486-cell exact-mode cost-validation pass (see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B), read-only `EXPLAIN` was additionally executed under forced `exact` mode across **all `486/486` cells** ($18 \times 3 \times 9$), confirming that our closed-form `Seq Scan + Top-k Sort` equation (9) matches PostgreSQL 18.4's displayed startup and total costs in **`486/486` cells (`100.0%`) with `0.0000` maximum cost difference (identical at two-decimal `EXPLAIN` precision)**, validating the estimated cost model across all 486 cells without claiming physical latency measurements for the 16 un-timed intermediate scales.
2. **Client Round-Trip Timing Stage (`9,900` trials) vs. Buffer-Diagnostic Stage (`540` `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` runs):** The client timing stage measures `planner_selected` across `P1`–`P9` (`8,100` trials) and `exact` and `forced_hnsw` at `P4` (`900` trials each) across 5 randomized passes per scale (preceded by 2 warm-up passes) within single-session windows (`12.07 s` at $1{,}001$, `16.36 s` at $5{,}000$, `32.47 s` at $10{,}000$). Because `time.perf_counter()` brackets `cur.execute(..., prepare=False)` and `cur.fetchall()`, this stage captures **end-to-end client round-trip latency** (including driver serialization, Windows loopback IPC, server query planning, execution, and result transfer). The separate diagnostic stage runs `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS, SETTINGS, FORMAT JSON)` at Profile P4 (`540` runs in the supplementary repositories, Section VI-B) to record root-node 8 KiB shared-buffer block counts. Because `TIMING OFF` was not specified during the diagnostic pass, its `execution_time_ms` includes per-node `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` instrumentation overhead from a separate 1-pass run (in `1,001 fp32_extended exact`, diagnostic median execution time is `10.489 ms` vs. client round-trip median `5.127 ms`); therefore, diagnostic timings are reported strictly as **instrumented diagnostic measurements**, not as pure server wall-clock speedup ratios. Across the 9 Profile P4 `forced_hnsw` cells, the inferred difference between client median latency and diagnostic server (`Planning + Execution`) median time spans **`1.11–1.64 ms`** (`1.19–1.68 ms` relative to `Execution Time` alone), rather than a fixed constant.
3. **Statistical Inference ($N = 20$ query blocks):** Averaging the 5 passes into $N = 20$ query-level means, we evaluate (i) tie-corrected Friedman omnibus tests ($\chi^2_8$, BH FDR-adjusted [17], [18]) across `P1`–`P9` (which act as within-session negative controls at $N \ge 5{,}000$ where all 9 profiles execute the identical HNSW plan), (ii) paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests [17] with Holm adjustment [19] and rank-biserial $r_{\text{rb}}$ [20] across representations under P4 `forced_hnsw`, and (iii) P4 `exact` vs. `forced_hnsw` paired Wilcoxon tests (BH-adjusted) alongside 30,000-resample query-cluster bootstrap 95% CIs. We report both **continuity-corrected (`+0.5`) asymptotic normal Wilcoxon $p$-values** ($z = (|W - \mu_W| - 0.5)/\sigma_W$) and **exact combinatorial Wilcoxon $p$-values** ($2^{20}$ sign assignments) consistently across all tables and text. For primary conventional `Recall@10` ($n = 20$ query-level observations per `exact` and `forced_hnsw` cell, as well as the $n = 19$ sensitivity subset excluding `Q9`), we compute percentile 95% confidence intervals (`2.5th` and `97.5th` order-statistic percentiles at indices `250` and `9,750`) by resampling the sorted query-level observations (`query_id` `0..19`) with replacement across `10,000` bootstrap replications using an independent `random.Random(42)` stream (`rng.choice`) per series (see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B). These intervals characterize finite-sample variability over our fixed topical 20-query workload (`q0`–`q19`) and do not establish generalization to an arbitrary universe of semantic queries (degenerating to `[1.0000, 1.0000]` in `exact` cells where all 20 queries achieve `1.0000`).

### D. Primary Conventional `Recall@10` Summary (RQ3)

Evaluating our primary retrieval metric—conventional ID-intersection `Recall@10` against the exhaustive exact FP32 reference set (see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B)—establishes two core findings. ## VI. Discussion, Dimensionality Regimes, Practical Guidelines, and Limitations

### A. Guard Boundaries, Analytical Extrapolation, and Dimensionality Regimes

Our 18-scale sweep and source-code analysis highlight three structural properties of `pgvector` 0.8.4's `startupPages > relpages && ratio < 0.5` heuristic:
1. **Parameter Dependence of the Lower `ratio < 0.5` Boundary ($N = 786 \to 787$):** Because $\text{ratio}(N)$ in (3) depends only on $N, m,$ and $ef_{\text{search}}$, the lower guard activation at $N = 786 \to 787$ is invariant to vector dimension $d$ but shifts whenever $m$ or `hnsw.ef_search` is modified (e.g., increasing `ef_search` above `40` increases `ratio` and pushes the `ratio < 0.5` threshold to a larger $N$).
2. **Analytical Projection of the Upper Guard Boundary ($N \approx 50{,}000$ on `FP32 Extended`):** Across our experimentally tested scales ($N \le 10{,}000$), `startupPages > relpages` holds for `fp32_extended` at every scale (`253.90 > 32` at $N = 5{,}000$, and `~270 > 64` at $N = 10{,}000$). However, because $\text{startupPages}(N) \approx 0.4985\,(48 + 54.152441\ln N)$ grows logarithmically while main-heap $\text{relpages}(N) \approx N / 157$ grows linearly, equating the two predicts an upper analytical crossing near **$N \approx 50{,}000$ (`~49.5k–50.2k`)**, above which `relpages` exceeds `startupPages` and deactivates $\Delta$ even on TOASTed `fp32_extended`. We emphasize that $N = 10{,}000$ is our maximum experimentally tested scale and that this $N \approx 50{,}000$ comparison is strictly an **analytical projection** beyond the measured scale range: under explicit projection assumptions at $N = 50{,}000$ ($\text{entryLevel}(50{,}000) = \lfloor \ln 50{,}000 / \ln 16 \rfloor = 3$, $C_{\text{cal}} = 54.152441134$, $\text{ratio}(50{,}000) = 0.01267835$, $\text{numIndexPages} \approx 0.4985\,N = 24{,}925$, and $\text{relpages} \approx \lceil N/157 \rceil = 319 > \text{startupPages} = 316.01$ so $\Delta = 0$), unadjusted `HNSW Limit` total cost remains well below `Seq Scan + Sort` under canonical Profile `P4` (`random_page_cost = 4.0, cpu_operator_cost = 0.0025`: `1,288.93` vs. `2,024.51`) and across `8/9` profiles (`P1–P6, P8, P9`), whereas only the extreme high-random-cost sensitivity probe `P7` (`random_page_cost = 8.0, cpu_operator_cost = 0.0025`) projects a higher unadjusted `HNSW Limit` cost (`2,572.90` vs. `2,024.51`) upon guard deactivation.
3. **Dimensionality Regimes Across Standard Embedding Sizes (`TOAST_TUPLE_THRESHOLD = 2,032 B`):** Comparing uncompressed datum size ($\text{VARSIZE}(d) = 8 + \text{bytes\_per\_elem}\cdot d$) and raw two-column tuple length ($t_{\text{len}}(d) = 28 + \text{VARSIZE}(d)$, with 8-byte aligned page-slot footprint $\text{MAXALIGN}(t_{\text{len}}(d)) = 32 + \text{VARSIZE}(d)$) against the default `2,032 B` threshold (`TOAST_TUPLE_THRESHOLD` / default `toast_tuple_target`) defines three qualitative dimensionality regimes (`~500`-D transition for `vector(d)` and `~1,000`-D transition for `halfvec(d)`). Because exact single-dimension integer cutoffs near $d \approx 500$ and $d \approx 1{,}000$ depend on table-specific non-vector columns and tuple header layout (`t_hoff`), Table V organizes the three regimes around standard embedding model dimensions ($d \in \{384, 512, 768, 1{,}024, 1{,}536\}$), highlighting the empirically verified split regime at $d = 768$.

### B. Practical Guidelines and Methodological Limitations

**Practical Guidelines:** (1) For 768-D embeddings (`Regime II`), adopting `halfvec(768)` (`fp16_extended`) cuts total storage by `55.21%–55.37%` and HNSW index size by `~50%`, packs 5 inline tuples/page, stores no out-of-line TOAST chunk data (`toast_table_bytes = 0 B`, `0` chunk pages) for the tested `fp16_extended` representation, and stays below the `4,096`-block `BAS_BULKREAD` threshold at $N = 10{,}000$; (2) when migrating a small 768-D table ($N < 2{,}000$) to `halfvec(768)` under Profile `P4`, the resulting `Seq Scan` selection is benign in warm cache (`1.674 ms` vs. `1.591 ms` $P_{50}$ at $N = 1{,}001$, NS) and automatically switches to HNSW at $N \ge 2{,}000$; and (3) on TOASTed `fp32_extended` tables ($m = 16, ef_{\text{search}} = 40$), the unforced planner selects `Seq Scan` across `8/9` tested cost profiles (`P1, P2, P4–P9`) when $N \le 786$ (where $\text{ratio}(N) \ge 0.5$ keeps the TOAST discount inactive) and under elevated `random_page_cost = 8.0` with default `cpu_operator_cost = 0.0025` (`P7`) up to $N = 1{,}250$, triggering full-table detoasting (`relpages + 3N` buffer hits and a `2.90×` client median slowdown at $N = 1{,}001$) unless index scans are enforced or cost parameters are adjusted.

**Methodological Limitations:** (1) *No Engine Patch:* We study unmodified PostgreSQL `18.4` and `pgvector` `0.8.4` with parallelism disabled (`max_parallel_workers_per_gather = 0`); (2) *Single-Session Warm Cache & Instrumentation:* Client round-trip timings reflect single-session warm-cache execution (`prepare=False`) with an inferred `1.11–1.64 ms` client-minus-server (`Planning + Execution`) difference (`1.19–1.68 ms` relative to `Execution Time` alone) across P4 `forced_hnsw` cells and transient Pass-2 spikes, while diagnostic-stage timings include `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` per-node timing overhead. Latency measurements characterize the observed benchmark runs; consistency of latency distributions across independent reruns was not established; (3) *Single HNSW Build & 20 Fixed Queries:* Each table uses one HNSW build ($m = 16, ef_{\text{construction}} = 64$); because repeated graph builds were not evaluated, the contribution of graph-construction variability to the observed `0.005` recall differences under `forced_hnsw` cannot be isolated, and inferences are conditioned on the 20 fixed queries (`q0`–`q19`); (4) *Duplicate-Vector Cluster:* The 28-chunk exact duplicate vector cluster in the prepared `BeIR/msmarco` slice drives the `Q9` boundary tie at $N = 10{,}000$ (Appendix A); (5) *Empirical Scale, Dimension Scope, and Nested-Prefix Sampling:* Empirical runs span $N \le 10{,}000$ at $d = 768$ (while $N \approx 50{,}000$ and Table V are analytical projections), and because all scales are constructed via deterministic nested prefix selection (`ORDER BY chunk_id ASC LIMIT N`) rather than independent random subsampling, corpus composition and local vector neighborhood density shift with $N$ (e.g., the recurring duplicate-vector cluster grows from `4` chunks at $N = 1{,}001$ to `12` at $N = 5{,}000$ and `28` at $N = 10{,}000$), so trends across $N$ reflect both relation cardinality and evolving prefix composition rather than isolating scale alone; (6) *Coupled Representation Variables:* Contrasting `fp16_extended` and `fp32_plain` simultaneously varies precision, tuple density (`5` vs. `2` rows/page), index size, and operator class without hardware performance counters; and (7) *Data and Code Availability, Supplementary Evidence Archive, and From-Scratch Reconstruction Boundaries:* The original research and development materials are maintained in the public `CompressionAwareRag` repository, specifically its `research/planner-sensitivity` branch (https://github.com/yoswinner/CompressionAwareRag/tree/research/planner-sensitivity). A separate curated publication and reproduction snapshot is maintained in the `pgvector-planner-sensitivity` repository (https://github.com/yoswinner/pgvector-planner-sensitivity). The former provides the broader research-development context, while the latter organizes selected materials associated with the accepted research release. The curated repository is currently private; access may be provided for academic review upon request. All provisioning, benchmark, statistical-analysis, bootstrap, tie-sensitivity, and figure-generation scripts together with all captured `EXPLAIN` plan trees, storage catalogs, client-latency logs, buffer diagnostics, and verification manifests (`45` scripts and dataset artifacts across the 3-scale evaluation, 18-scale sweep, 486-cell exact-mode cost validation, tie-sensitivity analysis, and publication figure suite) are archived across the supplementary repositories, enabling offline reproduction of the post-collection cost-model calculations, statistical tests, bootstrap confidence intervals, tables, and figures from the preserved evidence. This does not establish complete from-scratch reproduction of corpus construction, embedding generation, or the original database state. ## VII. Conclusion

We presented a closed-form C-source-to-`EXPLAIN` cost-model reconstruction and a multi-stage empirical evaluation (`4,860` baseline plan captures across `81` distinct settings, `972` 18-scale sweep captures plus `486` exact-mode validation captures across `486` settings, `9,900` timed client executions, and `540` buffer diagnostics) showing how FP16 vector representation (`halfvec(768)`) and PostgreSQL TOAST layouts (`STORAGE EXTENDED` vs. `STORAGE PLAIN`) govern query plan selection in PostgreSQL `18.4` with `pgvector` `0.8.4`. Across $N \in [500, 5{,}000]$, `pgvector`'s intentional TOAST-compensation guard (`startupPages > relpages && ratio < 0.5`) activates for out-of-line `fp32_extended` at $N = 786 \to 787$—where the isolated startup-cost dampening factor is `88.06×` display / `88.08×` unrounded (`788.15 -> 8.95` same-scale at P4; `87.93×` display / `87.95×` unrounded cross-scale) and root `Limit` total cost drops from `797.45` to `29.31`—flipping `7/9` profiles to HNSW while leaving `P7` in a harmful `Seq Scan` (`2.90×` client median slowdown at $N = 1{,}001$) until `[1250, 1500]`. Conversely, inline `fp16_extended` (5 tuples/page, `relpages = 201` at $N = 1{,}001$) and `fp32_plain` (2 tuples/page, `relpages = 501`) keep `relpages >= startupPages` (`0/162` guard activations each), raising P4 root `Limit` total cost from `30.37` (`fp32_extended`) to `422.90` (`13.92×` display / `13.93×` unrounded) and `844.90` (`27.82×`) at $N = 1{,}001$ and causing `5/9` profiles each to select `Seq Scan` (benign on `fp16_extended` at P4, `1.07×` median ratio, $p_{\text{adj}} = 0.2024$) before linear `Seq Scan + Sort` cost growth restores `27/27` HNSW selection by $N = 5{,}000$. Separating access-method selection from coordinate precision, HNSW index traversal delivers statistically significant client-latency speedups over exact sequential scans at $N \ge 5{,}000$ across all three representations (`15.76×` and `33.86×` client median speedups on TOASTed `fp32_extended`, `3.31×` and `18.59×` on inline `fp32_plain`, and `2.29×` and `5.15×` on inline `fp16_extended` at $N = 5{,}000$ and $10{,}000$, exact BH $p_{\text{adj}} = 2.45 \times 10^{-6}$, where the smaller ratio on `fp16_extended` reflects its faster inline exact scan rather than slower HNSW execution). Meanwhile, end-to-end `halfvec(768)` (`fp16_extended`) specifically cuts total relation storage by `55.21%–55.37%` and HNSW index size by `49.90%–49.98%`, stores no out-of-line TOAST chunk data for the tested `fp16_extended` representation (`toast_table_bytes = 0 B`, `0` chunk pages; rendering the $N = 1{,}001$ Profile P4 `Seq Scan` fallback benign at `1.674 ms` vs. `1.591 ms` $P_{50}$), and achieves `1.000 / 0.995 / 1.000` conventional `Recall@10` under exact scan (reflecting a 1-slot swap on `Q17` at $N = 5{,}000$) and `0.985 / 0.960 / 0.935` under P4 `forced_hnsw` versus `0.985 / 0.955 / 0.935` on `fp32_extended` and `0.985 / 0.955 / 0.930` on `fp32_plain` (`0.9737` and `0.9684` at $N = 10{,}000$ excluding `Q9`).

These findings apply within the boundary conditions of unmodified PostgreSQL `18.4` and `pgvector` `0.8.4`, single-worker warm-cache execution (`relallvisible = 0`), a single HNSW graph build per table ($m = 16, ef_{\text{construction}} = 64, ef_{\text{search}} = 40$), and 20 fixed topical `BeIR/msmarco` queries at $d = 768$ ($N \le 10{,}000$). For database administrators operating in the split dimensionality band (`Regime II`, $\sim\!500 < d < \sim\!1{,}000$, empirically verified at $d = 768$), `halfvec(768)` stores no out-of-line TOAST chunk data for the tested `fp16_extended` representation (`toast_table_bytes = 0 B`, `0` chunk pages) and avoids `relpages + 3N` sequential-scan detoasting while remaining benign during small-table `Seq Scan` fallbacks; meanwhile, on `vector(768)` tables under `STORAGE EXTENDED` ($m = 16, ef_{\text{search}} = 40$), unforced sequential scans occur when $N \le 786$ ($\text{ratio}(N) \ge 0.5$ across `P1, P2, P4–P9`) or when `random_page_cost = 8.0` is paired with default `cpu_operator_cost = 0.0025` (`P7` at $N \le 1{,}250$), where full-table detoasting degrades latency unless HNSW traversal is enforced or cost parameters are adjusted. For extension and optimizer designers, our source-level analysis demonstrates that keying TOAST compensation solely on main-heap `relpages` conflates compact inline representations with out-of-line TOASTed tables—and because PostgreSQL may allocate an empty `pg_toast` relation (`toast_table_bytes = 0 B`, `toast_index_bytes = 8,192 B`) even when no attributes are stored out-of-line, cost estimators should inspect effective per-column storage policy (`attstorage`) and actual out-of-line TOAST chunk usage (`pg_toast` chunk pages $> 0$) rather than mere `pg_toast` relation presence. Future work should extend this evaluation across multiple HNSW construction seeds, cold-cache NVMe I/O regimes, parallel query execution, and higher-dimensional embeddings ($d \ge 1{,}024$) where both FP32 and FP16 datums exceed `TOAST_TUPLE_THRESHOLD`.

## Appendix A: Secondary Analysis — Retrieval Boundary Ties, Exact Vector-Equality Cluster, and $\tau$-Tolerance Sensitivity

To support RQ3 without conflating distinct tie mechanisms or treating repeated planner profiles as independent queries, Table IV and Fig. 3 report query-level retrieval fidelity ($n = 20$ query-level observations per `(scale, representation, mode)` cell) along with 10,000-replication query-level percentile 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (`random.Random(42)` reset per series; see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B). Because the 9 planner cost profiles (`P1`–`P9`) reuse the exact same 20 queries (`q0`–`q19`), we do not pool or bootstrap $9 \times 20 = 180$ profile-query combinations as if they were independent observations; instead, planner-selected profile splits are reported separately in the table note. Formally, for corpus slice $\mathcal{C}_N$ of cardinality $N$ and query $q$, let $d_{\text{FP32}}(c, q)$ denote the exact FP32 cosine distance on `chunks_{N}_fp32_extended`, let $R_{\text{ref}}(q) = (c_{(1)}, \dots, c_{(10)})$ denote the exact FP32 reference top-10 sequence returned by `ORDER BY distance ASC LIMIT 10` under `exact` (`Seq Scan`) mode, and let $D(q) = d_{\text{FP32}}(c_{(10)}, q)$ denote the exact FP32 rank-10 cutoff distance. Given any candidate top-10 result set $S \subseteq \mathcal{C}_N$ ($|S| = 10$) returned by an evaluated configuration and a non-negative tolerance $\tau \ge 0$, we partition the reference boundary into the strictly closer set $B_\tau(q) = \{c \in \mathcal{C}_N \mid d_{\text{FP32}}(c, q) < D(q) - \tau\}$ and the disjoint boundary-tie set $T_\tau(q) = \{c \in \mathcal{C}_N \mid |d_{\text{FP32}}(c, q) - D(q)| \le \tau\}$, with $r_\tau(q) = 10 - |B_\tau(q)|$ available boundary slots (see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B). Conventional ID-intersection recall is $\text{Recall@10}_{\text{conv}}(S, q) = |S \cap R_{\text{ref}}(q)| / 10$, and tie-aware recall is $\text{Recall@10}_{\text{tie}}(S, q; \tau) = (|S \cap B_\tau(q)| + \min(|S \cap T_\tau(q)|, r_\tau(q))) / 10$. Because IEEE-754 single-precision (`binary32`) machine epsilon is $2^{-23} \approx 1.192 \times 10^{-7}$, $\tau = 10^{-7}$ and $\tau = 10^{-6}$ serve as operational numerical-equivalence thresholds (with $\tau = 10^{-6}$ above FP32 machine epsilon); across our dataset, all `6` rank-10 boundary-tie instances at $\tau \in \{10^{-7}, 10^{-6}\}$ involve duplicate embedding vectors equal under `pgvector`'s `'='` operator with zero cosine distance difference ($\Delta d = 0.0$), whereas $\tau = 10^{-5}$ acts as an additive near-tie tolerance that additionally credits `Q17` at $N = 5{,}000$ ($\Delta d = 1.665 \times 10^{-6}$ around $D = 0.45295169$).

1. **Duplicate Embedding Vectors Equal Under `pgvector`'s Vector Equality Operator (`=`) on `Q9` ($N = 10{,}000$):** For Query `Q9` (*"difference between supervised and unsupervised machine learning"*, `query_id = 9`), the rank-10 boundary distance at $N = 10{,}000$ is $D = 0.44327880887538984$. The strictly closer set is $B = \{8105, 5314\}$ ($|B| = 2$, with distances `0.432029` and `0.433520`; chunks `8105` and `5314` are distinct non-duplicate vectors with pairwise distance `0.3477`), while the boundary tie set $T$ contains $|T| = 28$ chunks (`[166, 631, 684, 970, 1135, 2139, 3024, 3177, 3243, 3882, 4130, 4837, 5443, 6623, 7014, 7433, 7617, 8192, 8621, 8634, 8886, 9261, 9281, 9395, 9398, 9627, 9813, 9828]`) competing for $r = 10 - 2 = 8$ slots (see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B). Read-only SQL verification (`SELECT (a.embedding = b.embedding), (a.embedding <=> b.embedding)` on `public.chunks_10k_fp32`) across all $\binom{28}{2} = 378$ pairs in $T$ confirmed that **`378/378` pairs return `True` under `pgvector`'s vector equality operator (`=`) with cosine distance `0.0`**. This establishes that the 28 chunks are **duplicate embedding vectors equal under `pgvector`'s vector equality operator** (forming an **exact vector-equality cluster**, without independently establishing raw binary byte-for-byte storage identity or identical original corpus text). Without a secondary SQL tie-breaker key, the three modes return `8105` and `5314` followed by 8 different members of this 28-chunk exact vector-equality cluster:
   - **Ground-Truth `exact` (`Seq Scan` + Top-10 Heapsort) returned order:** `[8105, 5314, 3024, 3177, 166, 2139, 631, 970, 684, 1135]` (bounded heapsort emits tied chunks in heap-insertion/sift order rather than ascending `chunk_id`).
   - **`FP32 Extended` `forced_hnsw` returned order:** `[8105, 5314, 8634, 8621, 8192, 7617, 7433, 7014, 6623, 5443]`.
   - **`FP16 Extended` `forced_hnsw` returned order:** `[8105, 5314, 9828, 9813, 9627, 9395, 9398, 9281, 9261, 8886]`.
   Because the 8 tied chunks returned by `forced_hnsw` do not overlap the 8 tied chunks retained by the bounded heapsort in `exact` mode, ID-intersection `Recall@10` scores `Q9` as `2/10 = 0.20` (widening the 20-query bootstrap CI at $N = 10{,}000$ to `[0.8450, 0.9950]`, compared with `[0.9368, 1.0000]` across the remaining 19 queries), whereas equivalence-class `Tie-Aware Recall@10` ($\tau \in \{10^{-7}, 10^{-6}\}$) credits any 8 members of the 28-chunk exact vector-equality cluster (`10/10 = 1.00`, accounting for the entire `+0.0400` delta at $N = 10{,}000$).
2. **Separating Cluster Presence (`11 / 2 / 1` Queries) from Actual Rank-10 Boundary Ties (`6` Instances Across `5` Queries) and Genuine $\epsilon$-Near-Ties (`Q17`):** A query can contain members of the recurring exact vector-equality cluster (`4` chunks `[166, 631, 684, 970]` at $N = 1{,}001$, `12` at $N = 5{,}000$, `28` at $N = 10{,}000$) in its top 10 without experiencing a rank-10 boundary tie if all cluster members fall strictly inside ranks 1–9 ($B$). Specifically (see Data and Code Availability, Section VI-B):
   - **Cluster presence in top-10:** Members of the exact vector-equality cluster appear in the top-10 of **`11` queries at $N = 1{,}001$** (`Q2, Q3, Q5, Q8, Q9, Q12, Q13, Q14, Q16, Q17, Q18`), **`2` queries at $N = 5{,}000$** (`Q3, Q9`), and **`1` query at $N = 10{,}000$** (`Q9`).
   - **Actual rank-10 boundary ties ($|T| > r$ at $\tau = 10^{-7}$ and $10^{-6}$):** Exactly **`6` query-scale instances across `5` unique queries** straddle rank 10: `3` at $N = 1{,}001$ (`Q5, Q13, Q16`), `2` at $N = 5{,}000$ (`Q3, Q9`), and `1` at $N = 10{,}000$ (`Q9`). For these 6 instances, `Tie-Aware Recall@10` at $\tau \le 10^{-6}$ evaluates equivalence-class membership over duplicate embedding vectors equal under `pgvector`'s vector equality operator rather than an approximate tolerance loosening.
   - **Genuine $\epsilon$-near-tie ($\tau = 10^{-5}$):** Relaxing $\tau$ from $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-5}$ adds exactly **`1` instance** (`Q17` at $N = 5{,}000$, bringing the total to `7` query-scale instances across `6` unique queries), where chunks `1571` and `2675` are distinct vectors separated in FP32 cosine distance by $\Delta d = 1.665 \times 10^{-6}$ around $D = 0.45295169$. Crediting `Q17` at $\tau = 10^{-5}$ (`0.9950 -> 1.0000` in `exact` mode and `0.9800 -> 0.9850` in `forced_hnsw` for `fp16_extended`) represents an explicitly tolerant / optimistic $\epsilon$-recall measure [23].
